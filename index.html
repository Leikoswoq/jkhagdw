REVERSE ENGINEERING · DESIGN STUDY

Behind the
beautiful chaos.

Wedding Dash is a scheduling puzzle disguised as a wedding reception. This notebook separates its publicly described behavior from the implementation choices in this playable reconstruction. No original executable, source code, sprites, dialogue, or soundtrack was supplied or extracted.

What the original asks you to balance

Observed systemPublicly described behavior · original PC game

Individual seatingGuests prefer particular neighbors. Seating order and remaining adjacent chairs matter.
Service sequenceCollect gifts, deliver them to the newlyweds, then serve appetizer, entrée, and dessert.
Action chainsBatch repeated actions to increase score. Guest impatience competes with waiting for a larger batch.

THE SPATIAL PUZZLE

A chair is a resource.

Keeping an adjacent seat free has a cost: one fewer person can enter service. The value of a seat changes as different guests arrive.

THE SCHEDULING PUZZLE

A batch is a gamble.

Waiting can produce a larger scoring chain, but it also consumes patience. The interesting decision is when to stop batching and rescue an urgent guest.

THE ATTENTION PUZZLE

Two queues, one player.

The workers can act in parallel, but the player must notice requests and assign both queues. A fast worker cannot fix an unnoticed emergency.

A practical guest state model

Arrival queue→Seated · gift→Appetizer→Eating→Main→Eating→Cake→Dance request→Celebrating

A waiting or seated guest can leave if patience runs out. A drink or song request temporarily interrupts the next meal request. Completed service refreshes patience. These are explicit reconstruction rules.

Guest = { id, archetype, seat, stage, nextStage, patience, maxPatience, eatTimer, extraRequest } Worker = { position, route[], currentTask, queue[], inventory[] } Task = { kind, targetId, expectedStage, destination } Each simulation tick: 1. Admit scheduled arrivals when the waiting area has room. 2. Update eating timers, patience, and emergency deadlines. 3. Advance both workers along their separate routes. 4. Revalidate and complete tasks that reach their targets. 5. Update stress, chain scoring, and end conditions.

Systems that stay separate

ModuleResponsibility in this implementation
SimulationDeterministic guest composition, timers, requests, stress, and end-of-level conditions.
Task schedulerEach worker owns a FIFO queue. Tasks are checked again on arrival so a departed guest cannot receive food.
NavigationA visibility graph routes workers around table rectangles; shortest-path search chooses a route.
ScoringSeparate seating, service, and dance chains. Emergency handling does not break a food chain.
PresentationSVG scenery and characters, DOM buttons and request bubbles, CSS motion, and Web Audio chimes.
PersistenceVersioned browser-local progress, best scores, unlocked stages, and purchased upgrades. Storage failures are handled.

Useful verification cases

Try leaving a guest before a queued delivery arrives; filling the tray with gifts before food pickup; pausing during a crisis; replaying a completed stage; clearing pending actions; and serving identical courses while Theo handles an emergency.

Reconstruction choices, made explicit

This is a compact playable interpretation of the core systems. It is not a binary-compatible port or an exact reproduction of the original campaign. All values in this table belong to this prototype.

AreaImplemented here
Campaign12 original receptions, 3 scene palettes, 6 original guest archetypes, and a separate endless mode. The third banquet table opens at reception 5.
PlanningThree wedding-detail choices. Each matching choice grants 75 starting points.
Inventory3 starting slots, upgraded to 5. A buffet visit fills free slots with the chosen course; food portions replenish over time. Gifts and music cards also occupy slots.
PointsSeating: 40, plus 40 × match chain. Gift delivery: 30 × service chain. Food: 50 × service chain. Dancing: 40 × dance chain. Drinks / song delivery: 100. Emergency: 150. All chains cap at ×5.
PatienceBase 48–76 seconds by archetype. Successful service refreshes it; eating pauses it. A missed preference costs 10 patience. Relaxed mode multiplies patience by 1.7.
Stress & failureUnresolved emergencies and very impatient guests raise stress. Expired emergencies add 24 stress; departed guests add 15 and cost 100 points. Fail at 100 stress or the displayed guest-loss limit.
GoalsCampaign pass goal: 250 × guests. Expert goal: 500 × guests. 1 medal for passing; a second for no departures; a third for expert. A stage ends after every guest has danced or left.
UpgradesWalking speed, tray slots, guest patience, and emergency resolution. Upgrades persist between receptions.
SimplificationsNo original story, campaign layouts, nine-crisis roster, reseating side requests, champagne system, or extracted art. Three original emergency categories use the same deadline model.

What would make the reconstruction closer?

Record a legal copy of the specific PC version at a fixed frame rate. Measure arrivals, travel times, patience loss, chain transitions, score deltas, and special-request rules one variable at a time. Store those observations separately from implementation guesses, then tune the configuration against repeatable recorded levels.
